Can Suggestions stay useful without taking over the draft?

This demo tests a versioned Suggestion contract and its lifecycle: quiet paragraph analysis, evidence-anchored structural analysis, explicit reassessment, duplicate suppression, stale-response rejection, and a firm boundary around Draft Priorities. It is intentionally not production code.

Decision being tested

Is this the smallest effective contract that can explain observable reader impact, preserve authorship, keep active feedback bounded, and supply evidence to—but never directly alter—the next requested Draft Snapshot?

Proposed Coaching Contract

Request: full Article, explicit analysis mode, and existing state

        
Response: dispositions plus bounded candidates

        
Deterministic application rules
Reject the whole response unless version, request ID, mode, and Article revision match.
Validate every block ID, block-local range, and exact quote against the live Article.
Require exactly one disposition for every affected active Suggestion.
changed_block: only passage scope, all primary evidence in the target block, 0..2 candidates.
whole_article: only structural scope, 2+ ordered evidence anchors, 0..3 candidates.
Reject low-impact candidates and questions attached above low confidence.
Compute a duplicate group from category + scope + anchored block IDs and overlapping ranges.
Keep one active concern per duplicate group; suppress a dismissed group until its source material or relevant Coaching Context changes.
Assign stable IDs and apply retain/revise/retire only after every validation passes.
Keep at most 8 active Suggestions: 5 passage + 3 structural, ordered by impact, confidence, then recency.
Never alter Draft Snapshot scores or Priorities from this response.

  1. Identify only observable, medium- or high-impact reader friction in the requested scope.

  2. Use the Writer’s purpose, audience, and Coaching Context as evidence. Do not silently override conflicting context.

  3. Separate scope from category. Categories are clarity, structure, concision, correctness, audience fit, and voice consistency.
    4. 
          
  4. Explain observation → reader impact → improvement direction. Do not write replacement prose or invent substance.

  5. For correctness, flag internal inconsistency, meaning-changing usage, or a need for verification; do not claim external fact-checking.
    6. 
          
  6. When intent may be deliberate, emit only if reader friction is observable, lower confidence, and make any intent question visible on open.
    7. 
          
  7. Reassess every supplied active Suggestion in scope. Prefer zero candidates to weak, repetitive, or unactionable advice.
    8. 
          
  8. Do not repeat active or dismissed concerns unless the supplied source material or relevant context changed materially.

Proposed verdict
Use one suggestion-analysis.v1 contract with two modes. Let the model judge reader impact and propose dispositions; let deterministic code validate, reconcile, cap, and preserve state. A requested Draft Snapshot may consume active Suggestions as secondary evidence, but remains the sole authority for up to three Priorities.
